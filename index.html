    The purpose of Langy

    A world of stories.
    More ways in.

    Language belongs to everyone. Langy brings reading and listening into one open workspace, so more people can spend more time absorbed in a story they understand.

    Open sourceSelf hostedBuilt around the reader

    Our story

    01 / Access

    The distance to a book is not the same for everyone.

    We live among more digital texts than any earlier generation. Access to reading remains profoundly unequal. UNESCO estimates that 739 million adults lacked basic literacy skills in 2024. Language, income and the availability of suitable material still shape who gets to participate. [1]

    For someone reading in another language, opening a book can introduce another set of obstacles. An unfamiliar expression breaks the thread. Finding a definition means leaving the page. Audio lives in a different app. A subscription adds one more expense.

    Langy’s purpose is to lower those barriers: to promote multilingualism and literacy globally, with particular concern for readers whose opportunities are limited by income. Software alone cannot resolve unequal education or access to technology. It can make the tools for reading more accessible, adaptable and shareable.

    02 / Why this exists

    When the message became the point.

    A note from the project’s creator

    I wanted to learn foreign languages from the time I was a kid. In high school, I kept returning to apps like Duolingo, hoping that this time I would finally get somewhere. The games gave me things to complete, but I was missing sustained, meaningful encounters with language in context. I was paying attention to how well I was doing, without making the progress I wanted.

    The turning point came when I started reading and listening for the message. I chose things I could understand and actually wanted to know about. My attention moved to what was happening in the story. As I stopped constantly judging my performance, I finally began making the progress I had wanted for years.

    I am now a Spanish teacher completing my MA in Spanish. I have come a long way through this approach. It has made learning enjoyable and sustainable for me, and I believe many others can find that same pleasure. Reading a few pages or listening during an ordinary day can fit around work and family. Progress can be substantial; the pace will differ for each person. Langy exists to make it easier to begin, and easier to keep wanting to return.

    03 / Foundations

    Make understanding easier.
    Let interest lead.

    These ideas inform Langy’s design. They come from distinct research traditions, and they do not amount to a clinical or educational evaluation of this app.

    Stephen Krashen · Compelling input

    Krashen proposes that understandable input becomes especially valuable when the message is compelling enough to absorb our attention. Langy puts the book first and offers help where comprehension breaks down. The aim is sustained contact with meaningful language, without turning every unfamiliar word into an assignment. [2]

    Beniko Mason & Ken Smith · Reading by choice

    Their account of Guided Self-Selected Reading emphasizes choosing understandable, enjoyable material and receiving guidance toward independent reading. In Langy, you can follow an interest, choose an easier book, or set a book aside without a penalty. Large amounts of reading become a possibility when returning to a book feels inviting. [3]

    Bill VanPatten · Attention to meaning

    VanPatten’s experiment on attending to form and content found limits on learners’ ability to process both simultaneously, particularly at earlier stages. This informs our decision to keep assistance brief and close to the passage. It does not mean that explicit instruction has no value; it means protecting the reader’s opportunity to understand the message. [4]

    Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi & Jeanne Nakamura · Flow

    Their account of flow describes deep involvement in the activity itself. Langy aims to reduce interruptions: help appears near the text, narration follows the page, and controls can disappear while you read. These are design choices intended to support absorption; the app cannot guarantee a flow state. [5]

    Alfie Kohn · The pleasure of the activity

    Kohn argues that controlling rewards and constant evaluation can shift readers’ attention away from reading itself. Langy’s home view keeps reading statistics out of the way unless you ask for them. A book may be unfinished. A reading day need not become a score. [6]

    Richard Ryan & Edward Deci · Autonomy and motivation

    Self-determination theory connects motivation to autonomy, competence and relatedness. For Langy, that suggests giving readers meaningful choices and enough support to understand what interests them. Progress information should serve a reader’s decisions, with no obligation to keep watching it. [7]

    What the evidence does—and does not—say

    Jeon and Day’s 2016 meta-analysis found benefits of extensive reading for reading proficiency across the studies they examined. Outcomes varied with the setting and program. [8] Research on rewards also needs care: Deci, Koestner and Ryan found that some tangible rewards reduced intrinsic motivation, while positive feedback could enhance it. [9]

    These findings do not establish that every paid or gamified app fails, or that metrics inevitably harm learning. Langy chooses a different emphasis: sustained enjoyment, reader autonomy, and attention to what you are reading. It makes no promise of a particular proficiency level or rate of progress.

    04 / The reading experience

    One place for the book.
    Help when you need it.

    Read an EPUB or PDF. Tap a word for help, listen alongside the text, ask a question about the current page, or request a brief explanation. Pick the language of that assistance. Keep your books and recordings together, and take downloaded book packs offline.

    Langy is intended to grow with a reader, from first accessible stories to demanding literature. Beginners may need short texts, graded readers or familiar stories with substantial support. More experienced readers may prefer authentic novels with occasional help. An authentic book is not automatically comprehensible, and an AI explanation is not a substitute for choosing material you can comfortably follow.

    Choose the amount of support that lets you enjoy the message. Vocabulary estimates and exposure counts are optional planning aids. They are not tests of your ability, and a recorded exposure is not proof that a word has been acquired.

    05 / Shared possibilities

    More languages.
    Fewer barriers.

    Langy’s software is open source under the MIT license. You can run it on your own server, inspect it, change it and share improvements. There is no compulsory Langy subscription. Hosting, electricity and any paid AI provider you choose can still have costs; the included local model does not require a paid AI service.

    Literature deserves broad availability across languages. Public-domain texts and openly licensed books can be preserved, translated and adapted more freely, within their respective terms. UNESCO’s recommendation on Open Educational Resources supports access and reuse through open licensing. A book’s availability on the web does not itself give it an open license. Supporting authors, translators and libraries belongs alongside widening access. [10]

    Langy can work alongside Anna’s Archive through optional search and import tools, connecting book discovery with a place to read. Langy is an independent project, with no official affiliation or endorsement. The integration is optional; your own files and other book sources work too.

    Our commitment is to global multilingualism and literacy: more people able to encounter ideas and stories beyond the languages or circumstances they were born into. A reader’s curiosity should be enough reason to open a book.

    06 / Your library workflow

    Your library, connected.

    Langy is designed to fit into the library you already use. Integrations with Anna’s Archive and Audiobookshelf, together with OPDS and KOSync, connect book discovery with reading and listening across your devices. The aim is a smooth library workflow that leaves more attention for the book itself.

    Anna’s Archive · Find your next book

    Optional search and import tools connect discovery to your Langy library. Choose an edition, bring in its EPUB or PDF, and begin reading with the support you need. Your own files and other book sources work alongside this integration.

    Audiobookshelf · Keep reading and listening together

    Import an existing audiobook and align it with your text, or export a complete Langy recording with its book and cover to the appropriate library folder. When both apps use the same recording, listening-position sync helps you continue between them.

    OPDS · Bring your library to your reader

    Langy publishes an OPDS catalog for compatible reading apps and devices. Browse categories and download books from the same personal collection, with no need to maintain a separate library for each reader.

    KOSync · Return to your place

    Built-in KOReader Sync functionality exchanges reading positions with compatible readers, including supported CrossPoint devices. Your place can follow you between Langy and an e-reader, so a change of device need not interrupt a story.

    Android APK · Take your books offline

    A downloadable Android APK lets you install Langy as an app. Connect it to your instance and save book packs, including audio if you want to listen, before going offline. Downloaded books, saved dictionary help and synchronized audio remain available without a live connection. New AI answers and other server tasks require a connection.

    These connections make Langy an adaptable part of your own reading environment. Use the tools and devices that suit your day, with fewer steps between finding a book and enjoying it. The setup guide explains how to connect them.

    Getting started

    01 / Installation

    A server, Docker, and your books.

    You need a computer or home server with Git, Docker Engine and the Docker Compose plugin. Keep enough disk space for NLP and AI models as well as your books. A modern server with ample RAM is preferable for local AI; speed and memory requirements depend on the selected model. The default stack uses CPU inference unless you configure GPU access.

    Install Docker using its official instructions, then run:

    git clone https://github.com/Attach7830/Langy-Reader.git
cd Langy-Reader
cp .env.example .env
nano .env

    Set a username and strong password before sharing access. Single-quote values containing $ in .env so Compose preserves them literally:

    READER_USER=reader
READER_PASSWORD='replace-with-a-long-unique-password'
READER_PORT=8088

    Start the stack:

    sudo docker compose up -d --build

    The first build downloads models and can take a while. Open http://localhost:8088 on that computer, or http://YOUR-SERVER-LAN-IP:8088 on your home network. Sign in with the credentials from .env. The stack includes the reader, a local language service, Ollama and a search service.

    By default your library is kept in a persistent Docker volume. If you choose another drive, use the storage migration instructions in the repository README before changing READER_DATA_SOURCE.

    02 / AI options

    Choose where language help runs.

    Included local model

    Choose Bundled Ollama in Settings → AI connection. The Compose stack includes a downloadable model, with no paid inference API required. CPU responses can be slow. This is the simplest way to start without another AI server.

    Your own AI server

    Choose Existing Ollama server, enter its reachable base URL and select a model. A server on another computer usually needs its LAN IP and port, such as http://192.168.1.20:11434. For a service on the Docker host, try http://host.docker.internal:11434. Inside a container, localhost refers to that container. See Ollama’s API documentation.

    A hosted API

    Choose OpenAI-compatible API. Langy calls Chat Completions; select a text-chat model that supports that API and the provider’s standard completion parameters. A paid service is optional. Provider access, free allowances and prices can change.

    03 / Without a local LLM server

    Connect an API in a few steps.

    1. Create an account with a provider below. Read its pricing and data-use terms, then create an API key. Add credits or billing if the provider requires them. A chat-app subscription does not necessarily include API access.
    2. Open Settings → AI connection, choose OpenAI-compatible API, and enter the base URL. Use the base address shown below; do not append /chat/completions.
    3. Paste the API key into the password field. Select Find available models and choose a compatible text-chat model, or enter its exact model ID from the provider’s catalog.
    4. Select Save settings. Try a short page summary and check that it returns in your chosen summary language. Model discovery confirms a connection; a successful summary also confirms generation support.

    Save answers, avoid repeat requests

    Save and reuse AI answers is on by default in Settings → Language help. Completed word help, summaries, follow-up explanations and book questions are stored on your instance, separately for each account. Reading estimates and discovery generations use the same cache. An identical request can be reused across sessions and devices; a different model or context generates a fresh answer. You can clear saved answers or turn saving off.

    This is full-answer reuse, separate from any provider-side prompt caching. New requests may still incur charges. Keys stay in your server configuration, not in book packs or source control. Excerpts and questions are sent to your selected provider when generation is needed. Your server administrator controls stored data. AI can make mistakes; check consequential claims against reliable sources.

    If the connection fails
    • Unauthorized: check the API key, billing and provider selection.
    • Model not found: choose an exact model ID available to that account.
    • Unavailable or timeout: check that the reader container can reach the URL, then try a smaller or faster model.
    • Unsupported parameter or empty answer: choose a model that supports this provider’s Chat Completions interface. Provider compatibility varies.

    04 / Your first reading session

    Pick a book you want to return to.

    In Settings → Language help, choose your reading languages, native language and the languages for translations and summaries. Add an EPUB or PDF; title, author and language can be detected and edited afterward. Choose a collection, and mark a book Currently Reading to keep it near the front of your library.

    On a touch screen, single-tap a word for help. Swipe left or right to turn a page; double-tap an outer edge if enabled. A blank-center tap toggles fullscreen. Double-tap a word to play audio from that word, or double-tap blank center space to play or pause. Swipe up to toggle the audio toolbar open or closed without pausing. Swipe down to hide open controls, or open sections and expandable bookmarks when controls are hidden. Select Audio to toggle playback controls. Pinch EPUB text on a touch screen or desktop trackpad to adjust that device’s font size. Phone/tablet and desktop sizes stay separate. After a hyperlink, use Back to text.

    Select a phrase and choose Explain phrase for its meaning in context. On a keyboard, arrows turn pages, Ctrl + Left/Right skips sections, F toggles fullscreen and S toggles the summary. With audio enabled, Space plays or pauses. Hotkeys can be changed in Settings. Ask a question in the summary panel when a passage needs more explanation.

    Your account and sharing

    The configured server login becomes the administrator after upgrading. In Settings → Account, administrators can add users and reset or disable accounts. Each user starts with a private library. Enable Share with other users on this server during import or in Edit book details to share a book; only its owner can change or remove it. Uncheck Show shared books in the library to hide received books. Your preferences, reading history, bookmarks and progress stay private. Sign out from Account before another person uses your device.

    Listening and offline reading

    Use Audio in the reader to open playback controls. Upload your own audiobook files, including chapter recordings, and synchronize them with the book. In Import book, you can schedule automatic audiobook generation after a delay if no audiobook exists. To generate narration, connect a compatible Kokoro server under Settings → Narration; Kokoro itself is optional and is not included in this stack. Choose a voice that supports the book’s language.

    Book packs download through a background queue. Completed packs show Downloaded in the library. Offline book packs are available in the Android APK. Download and open a book once in the app before going offline; include its recording if you want offline listening. AI generation still needs a reachable model server. Reading progress and supported queued changes synchronize when your instance becomes reachable again.

    Open Process queue to see progress. Completed tasks disappear automatically; failed tasks remain available to retry. In the Android app, configure your instance under Settings → Devices. The app uses the same server; private network addresses need a VPN or another reachable route when you leave home.

    Bring in a book from the web

    Choose Import book → Web link / free book catalogues. Search Gutenberg by title or author, or paste a direct public HTTPS EPUB/PDF link from a source such as Standard Ebooks or OAPEN. Review its language, Langy folder and optional audiobook delay, then import. Processes shows the download while you continue reading.

    Covers and free fonts

    In Import book or Book details, Find covers automatically searches your title and author. Automatic cover search combines Open Library and Google Books without requiring a key. You can also choose either source directly. An optional Google Books key in Settings → Book sources → Cover search can provide your own Google quota. Covers from both sources can be saved with the book. The optional Amazon connector needs approved Creators API credentials and shows live previews linked to the source edition. In Settings → Appearance → Imported fonts, Download a free font searches Google Fonts through Fontsource, or accepts a direct font-file link from supported official sources. Choose the language subset you need, download, then save settings.

    Your folder, your audiobook destination

    Edit a Langy folder and link its Audiobookshelf library and folder. The link applies to books already there and future additions. Every export follows that book’s current link. Unmapped folders wait for a destination; they do not use an unrelated default. Old copies in ABS are preserved if you change a folder link.

    Use a process’s small × to cancel pending work. Existing books and saved audio are retained. If an ABS upload’s outcome is uncertain, check it before retrying.

    Reading tools / Scanned books

    Make a scanned page readable.

    PDF books can gain searchable text without creating another library entry. Choose Scan PDF with OCR from a book’s actions. Match its simplified or traditional Chinese script; select a vertical model only for vertical text. The original file stays backed up.

    Built-in OCR runs in the language service. For the optional Chinese-focused PaddleOCR service on the same host, run:

    docker compose -f compose.yaml -f compose.ocr.yaml up -d --build paddle-ocr

    In Settings → Devices → PDF OCR, choose PaddleOCR, enter http://paddle-ocr:8000, then save and test. This service uses CPU inference and local, verified models. Use a modern AVX-capable Linux machine with sufficient memory. Scans preserve the visible PDF; digital-text pages are retained.

    On another machine, set LANGY_OCR_BIND to its private LAN/VPN address and set LANGY_OCR_API_KEY in its private .env. Start it with docker compose -f compose.ocr.yaml up -d --build paddle-ocr, then enter that machine’s reachable URL on port 8093 and the same key in Langy. Use the supplied PDF service, rather than a generic PaddleOCR JSON endpoint. The README covers limits and setup.

    In Settings → Appearance, cover caching is on by default. It saves up to 120 covers per account on this device and refreshes changed covers. Clear the cache or turn it off whenever you like; signing out clears it too. Supporting browsers need HTTPS or localhost for this cache.

    05 / Ongoing care

    Keep the books.
    Keep control.

    Back up the entire library data volume or bind-mounted data directory, plus your private .env. Stop the reader during a filesystem copy so SQLite databases and their journal files stay consistent. Keep API keys, passwords and Android signing keys out of Git.

    For remote access, use a VPN or an HTTPS reverse proxy/tunnel with Langy’s login enabled. The optional Cloudflare connector joins the same Compose network; route the hostname to http://reader:8080. Follow the tunnel instructions in the README to store its token locally.

    To update an existing reader without rebuilding language models:

    cd Langy-Reader
git pull --ff-only
sudo docker compose up -d --build --no-deps reader

    If you use the tunnel overlay, use the same Compose files and environment file you used to start it. Reload Langy afterward. Read sudo docker compose logs --tail=80 reader when an update fails.

    The public demo is for trying Langy. Shared AI can be slow; connecting a capable personal AI server to your own installation improves responsiveness. Demo upload limits and expiry are shown in its library.

    Separate accounts and library limits

    Open Settings → Account as an administrator to create users, reset passwords or revoke browser sessions. Each user has a private library and can choose which books to share. Account permissions include a saved-media allowance, expiry, remembered-login duration, browser-session cap and OPDS/KOReader access. Limits preserve existing files when lowered. Readers can save a private Anna’s Archive membership key; shared-key access requires an administrator’s permission.

    Choose the most complex material you understand consistently across several unfamiliar examples. Consider the message and relevant details, rather than recognizing a few words. These optional checks are broad self-estimates.

    How this estimate works

    Encountered vocabulary, repetition, active reading time and estimated outside hours describe your exposure. The tentative ranges use your comprehension checks and broad ACTFL skill descriptions. Input hours add context; they do not automatically award a level. More exposure does not guarantee comprehension or retention.

    Mandarin uses distinct Han characters; other languages use lemmas. These are different units, and they are never combined. English word-family thresholds are not interchangeable with either measure. Your exposure threshold is a personal setting, not a validated mastery cutoff.

    The graph uses saved first encounters and daily observations. Changing a threshold changes the familiar-vocabulary measure. Correcting estimated hours can move the hours line downward. Activity is a record of pages viewed or books marked read; it does not test attention. .

    Reading and listening can develop at different rates. This is not a formal ACTFL assessment, a CEFR certificate, or an estimate of speaking and writing ability.
